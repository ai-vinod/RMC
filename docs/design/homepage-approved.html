Answers to your two questions

Doctor photos go in all three places, cropped differently. Homepage: small circular headshots, as above — faces do more for trust than any line of copy. Service page: one larger photo of that doctor. About: all four at a larger size with the full bios. The two you sent are in; Dr. Sudharsan and Dr. Niranjani are still initials until their photographs arrive.

Google reviews are hand-picked and fixed. Five of the best, typed in from the public listing with the reviewer's first name, and a button through to the full listing. No API, no key, no cost, nothing tied to the hosting. Refresh them once or twice a year in a maintenance month. One consequence: the proposal says the reviews are pulled live and refresh on their own, which will no longer be true — that line needs changing before it goes out.

Video inside a Google review cannot be pulled. The API returns review text, author, rating and date, not reviewer-uploaded video, and media hosted on Google Maps cannot be embedded reliably. If a patient has left one, ask them for the original file, put it on the clinic's YouTube, and link it like the rest. You need their written consent for a medical testimonial anyway.

The rail is built for vertical. Cards are 9:16 because that is what phone footage, Shorts and Reels all are. Two show on a phone, four or five on a desktop, and it takes three to six links. Tapping one opens the player rather than loading five embeds into the page, which would otherwise ruin the mobile speed score.

Still outstanding on testimonials: these were dropped from launch on 4 Oct and this brings them back, so the compliance point returns with them. Get it in writing from Dr. Madhavan that he has chosen to publish patient videos, and I will update the requirements document.
